Only two days had passed since the last armoured vehicle disappeared into the dark rift, leaving behind a world doomed to die.

The new camp had not yet become a real home. As always, the heavy all-terrain vehicles stood in a tight ring, forming an improvised fortress. Soldiers were already installing steel shields between them, mechanics were checking the engines after the hurried evacuation, and cooks were lighting the field stoves. People worked quickly, hardly speaking. Everyone was busy with their own task, but from time to time they still looked back, as though unconsciously expecting to see on the horizon the same wall of fire from which they had miraculously escaped.

Even the children were unusually quiet. The day they had left the war-torn plane had left too much in everyone’s memory. No one had forgotten how the last vehicles passed through the portal under a hot rain of ash.

The Major carefully surveyed the camp. Once he was sure everyone had found something to do and all was calm, he gestured for Isaac to come over. Isaac approached almost immediately.

“While everyone is settling in, I want you to find a plane where we could live,” the Major said calmly. “There must be water, food, and inhabited settlements. Look for roads and possible threats, but don’t go too far. If anything seems suspicious, return immediately.”

“Understood.”

Isaac nodded briefly and, within a few minutes, was standing beside a small reconnaissance armoured vehicle. Two operators were launching drones, while three more scouts checked their equipment. Nayrin watched the preparations in silence.

“Want to come with us?” Isaac asked.

“If the Major doesn’t object.”

“There’s no such thing as too many eyes on a reconnaissance mission,” the Major said with a shrug. A few minutes later, five people had disappeared into the dark passage.

The first world was silent.

They found themselves in the middle of a plain stretching all the way to the horizon. The ground beneath their feet was white with a thick layer of salt, cracked as though this had once been the bed of an enormous sea. Here and there, dried tree trunks rose from the ground, long stripped of bark and branches. The wind drove fine salt dust between them, but there was no other sound.

Isaac stared into the distance through his binoculars for a long time.

“No birds… no insects…”

One of the scouts crouched, touched the ground, and rubbed the white crystals between his fingers.

“Nothing has lived here for a very long time.”

They walked several more kilometres. Ahead, they could make out the remains of a city. Only concrete frames remained of the buildings, almost completely buried in salt. Even the metal structures had crumbled with age.

“There’s no point going farther,” Isaac said quietly. No one objected.

The second world greeted them with a dense green forest.

For the first few minutes, it even seemed they had finally found a place suitable for life. Leaves rustled overhead, a stream murmured nearby, and the air smelled of damp earth. But that impression did not last long.

The forests here belonged not to trees, but to fungi. Everything was covered with enormous black mushrooms. They grew on trunks and stones, straight out of the ground, their thick greyish threads intertwining. Some caps were as tall as a person.

One of the scouts carelessly brushed against a mushroom with his shoulder. It trembled slightly, and the next moment a dense dark cloud of spores burst from beneath its cap.

“Back!” Isaac ordered sharply.

Everyone almost simultaneously sealed the valves of their protective suits. The cloud slowly dispersed into the air, but the analyser had already begun to beep loudly. One of the scouts studied the readings carefully.

“Without a filter… your lungs would be overgrown with these fungi within a few hours.”

They did not stay any longer.

The third portal opened in the middle of a wide meadow.

The air smelled of freshly cut hay. A river murmured nearby, and beyond it stretched fields divided by stone walls. The sun was already sinking towards the horizon, lighting a small village nestled against the foot of a hill. A thin thread of smoke rose above the thatched roofs, cows grazed peacefully in a pasture, and the steady ringing of a hammer came from the direction of a forge.

Several villagers were returning from the fields, leading carts loaded with sheaves of wheat. No one was in a hurry. There was no roar of engines, no gunfire, no smell of burning. Isaac remained silent for a long time.

“It seems… they don’t even know what war is.”

Nayrin smiled for the first time in many days.

“Or they haven’t seen one for a very long time.”

They did not enter the village. They simply watched the people from a distance for several hours. The villagers worked, children played by the river, and women went about their household tasks. When the sun touched the horizon, the bell of a small wooden church rang above the village, and people began making their way home.

“I think we’ve found what we were looking for,” Isaac said quietly.

They returned to the camp after dark. The Major listened to the report without interrupting once. When Isaac had finished, he remained silent for a long time, studying the map of the branch of worlds they had begun assembling only a few days earlier.

“Then it’s decided,” he finally said. “We’re moving to that world.”

There were no objections. Before dawn, the column set off again. This time, it moved in a very different way. The large portals were no longer opened only by Nayrin, Lior, Marta, and Olena. Isaac and several others who had learned over the past few days to hold the space open worked alongside them. They took turns, helped those who were running out of strength, and within a few hours the last armoured vehicle had emerged onto the broad green meadow of the new world.

By evening, the familiar fortress of heavy vehicles had risen once again in the middle of the plain. Tents were stretched between the armoured vehicles, the kitchens were running, mechanics were working on the equipment, and children who only recently had watched a wall of fire were chasing one another between the machines again.

For the first time in a long while, everyone felt that the endless flight might finally be over. That was why the first scream came so unexpectedly that almost no one paid attention to it at first…

The camp was noisy. Dinner was just being served at the field kitchen, mechanics were finishing the replacement of a damaged wheel on one of the armoured vehicles, and nearby several children were laughing and chasing one another between the tension cables. After two days of continuous work, people had finally allowed themselves to relax, and a brief cry was easily lost among dozens of other sounds.

Only when the scream came again, much louder and more desperate, did people begin to look around.

A man who had been carrying wooden crates to the storage area only a few minutes earlier now stood motionless in the passage between the vehicles. A crate lay at his feet, with metal parts scattered around it, but he was not even looking at them. His gaze was fixed straight ahead, on a patch of evening air where there was nothing at all.

“Hey…” a companion called cautiously. “Are you all right?”

The man did not answer.

He slowly stretched out his hand, as though trying to touch something only he could see. The air in front of his palm suddenly shuddered. At first, no one thought much of it. Over the past few days, portals had become an ordinary part of camp life. They were opened dozens of times every day, and people had almost stopped noticing the familiar trembling of space.

That was exactly why no one understood that this time things were different. None of the people who knew how to open passages between worlds was standing nearby. The dark fissure appeared abruptly, and a moment later the man was being forcibly sucked into the opening.

It all happened so quickly that no one even had time to move. An invisible force dragged the man straight towards the dark rift. He desperately grabbed the edge of an armoured vehicle, his fingers slipping off the smooth metal, and a moment later the portal swallowed him without a trace.

The darkness closed at once, and people rushed to the place where the tragedy had just happened. Lior was the first to reach it. He crouched and touched the grass with his palm, as though hoping to find some trace, but all he could see was trampled earth and the metal parts scattered from the overturned crate.

“He couldn’t have opened a portal himself,” Nayrin said quietly, staring at the empty space. “He tried many times, but nothing ever worked.”

Marta was about to answer when another human scream tore through the camp’s silence. This time it came from the field kitchen. A young woman who had been serving dinner to the children only a moment earlier was stumbling backwards, unable to take her eyes off the air in front of her.

And before the eyes of hundreds of people, space began to part for the second time… This time, no one stood by and watched.

The woman backed away in short steps, instinctively holding her hands out in front of her. Her face was frozen in pain, as though something were tearing her apart from within. She could not even scream.

“Don’t move!” Lior shouted as he ran towards her.

But it was too late. As soon as the portal opened fully, the woman jerked violently. An invisible force yanked her forward so hard that she lost her balance and fell to her knees. Her fingers dug convulsively into the earth, tearing up clumps of black soil along with the grass, but it did no good. She was being dragged towards the dark boundary, quickly and inexorably.

At that very moment, Isaac rushed past her. He did not hesitate. Years of war had trained his body to act faster than thought could form. In one movement, he dropped beside her, wrapped his arms around her waist, and tried to pull her back. For an instant, it even seemed that he had succeeded.

The woman was almost beyond his reach when the air around them suddenly shuddered with renewed force. Isaac felt the ground slipping from beneath his knees. Everything lurched forward. He managed only to hold the woman more tightly, and then the darkness swallowed them both. The portal closed soundlessly.

The Major slowly lowered the hand with which he had been ordering people to stand back only a moment earlier. His face showed that he was desperately trying to find an explanation for what had happened.

“Nayrin…” he said quietly. “Can you…”

“No.”

Nayrin answered before the Major had finished his question.

He was already standing where the portal had just disappeared. Space was perfectly still. There was no trace, no sensation he could grasp.

“I don’t know where they went, or even what that was.”

Lior silently walked in a circle around the place where they had vanished. Marta and Olena exchanged glances. In all their years of travelling, they had seen countless strange worlds, but never before had a portal opened by itself and chosen a person.

Second after second passed. It seemed everything was over. Some people even began returning to their tasks, though they now worked in silence, constantly looking over their shoulders. Then the air above the same spot shuddered again. The dark fissure opened as unexpectedly as it had vanished. A moment later, a human body was literally thrown out of the darkness.

Isaac hit the ground hard and rolled several metres across the grass. His protective suit was blackened, in places covered with blisters of melted composite. One sleeve had burned away completely, exposing an arm with almost no uninjured skin left. Heat radiated from him as though he had just crawled out of a blazing furnace.

Olena was already running towards him, opening her medical kit as she moved. Marta dropped to her knees beside him, helping turn him onto his back.

Isaac was conscious. He was breathing heavily, but his eyes remained wide open. They stared somewhere far away, through the people and the camp, as though he could still see the world from which he had just returned.

Nayrin crouched beside him.

“Isaac… What’s there?”

Isaac looked straight into Nayrin’s eyes. His chest rose and fell heavily beneath the blackened remains of his protective suit, and his fingers clenched the grass convulsively, as though he could still feel the scorching ground beneath him. He tried to speak several times, but only a rasp came from his throat. Olena was already applying cooling gel to his exposed burns, while Marta helped cut away the damaged armour plates. Yet Isaac himself seemed unaware of the pain or the people around him. His entire being was still there, in the world from which he had just escaped.

Nayrin was about to place a hand on his shoulder when something suddenly changed between them. It was not like an ordinary mind-link. No one was trying to send a thought or address another person. Isaac’s consciousness, exhausted by pain and the horror he had experienced, seemed to have lost its last defence, and everything he had just seen poured out in one powerful stream. Nayrin did not even have time to resist. The camp disappeared before his eyes, along with the people, the evening sun, and the green meadow. In their place appeared another world—the one they had left only two days earlier.

The flames were no longer advancing in separate patches. They had become the land itself. A continuous crimson-black wave rolled all the way to the horizon, above which fiery whirlwinds rose as high as multi-storey buildings. The air had lost its transparency. It glowed with heat and caught fire even before the flames touched the bare ground. A constant rumble came from every direction, and these were not the explosions of war. It was the planet itself roaring as it tore apart beneath the onslaught of the fiery elements.

Isaac was running. He could barely feel his own body anymore. Through the smoke ahead, he could still make out the woman he had followed into the portal. She was very close, reaching out her hand towards him, but every step he took only seemed to increase the distance between them. The hot air distorted her outline, and a moment later her silhouette dissolved into the flames. Isaac kept running anyway, until he understood that no one could be saved. Then he opened a portal at random, simply to escape that hell. At the last moment, a wave of heat engulfed him with such force that even his protective suit began to melt against his body.

The image faded as abruptly as it had appeared. Nayrin drew a sharp breath, as though he too had just escaped the scorching air. He saw the Major, Lior, Marta, Olena, and several soldiers standing around Isaac. Their faces made it clear that they had experienced the same thing. No one asked anything. The fact that Isaac had used the mind-link for the first time to transmit an information packet went unnoticed. There would be time for that later.

People silently looked at one another, trying to comprehend what they had seen, when a scream suddenly came from the opposite end of the camp. This time it was not alone. Two more people screamed almost simultaneously. Then several others.

Dark rifts began opening one after another between the armoured vehicles. They appeared without any pattern: beside the kitchen, near the infirmary, right in the passages between the vehicles. People still did not understand what was happening. Some stepped back; others hurried closer, thinking that one of the travellers was opening a portal. Only when the first person was literally torn from the crowd and dragged into the darkness did true panic sweep through the camp.

The Major was still trying to issue orders, but his voice was already lost among dozens of others. Soldiers rushed towards their families. Mothers pressed their children against themselves, fathers grabbed them in their arms and tried to move away from the dark rifts that were opening without warning. Some people managed to hold on for a few seconds, but the invisible force was stronger. Slowly and inexorably, it tore people away from the ground, from the hands of their loved ones, from life itself. A moment later, the portal closed without a trace, leaving behind only silence and the despair of those who had been too late to help.

Only then did several scouts who had already learned to open portals understand what was happening. Without consulting one another, they began creating passages back to the world they had seen through Isaac’s eyes only minutes earlier. They were certain that someone could still be saved there.

“Don’t open them!” the Major’s voice cut through the general uproar. “No one goes back!”

But it was already too late.

One young officer had almost finished opening a passage when Lior shoved him away from the space that was already beginning to part. They both fell to the ground. The officer struggled, trying to reach the portal again.

“My wife is there!” he rasped. “She’s there, understand? Let me go, you idiot!”

Lior did not answer. He only pressed him harder against the ground, knowing that cruelty was the only way to save another life.

The same thing was happening all around them. People were no longer fighting for themselves. They rushed towards the places where they had last seen their children, parents, and friends, and each time they were only a moment too late.

It did not last long. As unexpectedly as they had begun to open, the last portal closed, and the camp sank into an unnatural silence.

Between the rows of armoured vehicles, the tents remained standing. Dinner pots were still steaming beside the kitchens; abandoned belongings, children’s toys, tools, and open ammunition crates lay scattered across the grass. But almost no people remained. Of the more than a thousand people they had saved from fiery death only three days earlier, little more than a hundred were left in the camp.

Nayrin slowly looked around the deserted camp. He could hear no crying, no voices. It was as though his ears had been stuffed with cotton. He could see people shouting and weeping, but all around him there was complete silence. It seemed that even the mind-link, which that morning had been filled with thousands of familiar presences, had suddenly emptied. The world had become strangely quiet.

It was in that silence that he saw everyone’s mind-link for the first time. Some were clear as fresh snow, others black as soot. Most of the people’s mind-links around him shimmered in different colours and changed shape every second. The one thing they all had in common was a sense of unity. All those mind-links seemed to come from one place: their home plane, which they had left ten years earlier.

Nayrin could already see that only they remained. All the people from the plane that was burning had returned to meet their deaths on their home planet.

A cautious thought began to form: since they had not gone home in the same way to die in the nuclear fire, perhaps their world was still alive and was not calling them back. But that thought quickly vanished in the face of the Major’s mind-link.

He stood silently in the middle of the camp, staring at the empty passages between the tents. His mind-link was so alien that Nayrin began to doubt whether this man had ever lived in the Path Cities or metropolises of their world at all.

Nayrin carefully examined what had appeared before him and understood that the Major might once have been one of their own, but he had changed so profoundly that a shiver ran through the boy’s body. He could almost physically sense… no, not the Major’s words or thoughts. His mood.

All the Major’s feelings now revolved around a single thought: the future empire, whose leader he already imagined himself to be, had collapsed in an instant. He was mourning not the people who had died, but the subjects who had failed to live up to his expectations. This struck the boy so deeply, amid the grief and desperate cries of people who had suddenly lost their loved ones, that it turned him away from this terrible man.

Nayrin would no longer walk the same path as the Major.
