The air above the wide field shivered almost imperceptibly, as though from summer heat, although the day was cool. A moment later, a narrow dark fissure opened soundlessly in the middle of the scorched earth. There were no flashes or rumbling. Space silently parted, revealing a passage to another world.

Nayrin was the first to emerge from the portal.

He did not hurry to take another step. Standing still, he carefully surveyed his surroundings, allowing his eyes to adjust to the new landscape. Before him lay an enormous field that had long since ceased to be a field. The ground was thick with craters from explosions, and old trenches ran between them, in places almost levelled with the earth. Burnt trees, stripped of branches, stood like black fragments, their trunks riddled with so many bullets that hardly any bark remained. Here and there, among the yellowed grass, lay the remains of concrete shelters, pieces of rusted machinery, and twisted tangles of barbed wire. Everything around them told the same story: war had arrived here long ago and had no intention of ending.

Once he was sure there was no one nearby, Nayrin briefly waved his hand. Lior, Marta, and Olena emerged from the dark passage one after another. A single glance around was enough for each of them to understand that remaining in the open was dangerous.

“Over there,” Nayrin said quietly, pointing towards a long ravine running alongside the field.

The four of them quickly crossed the open ground and descended into it. The slopes were thick with tall dry grass and young bushes, so within a few minutes they were well hidden from prying eyes. Only then did they allow themselves a short rest and take a closer look at the new world.

“It seems they’re constantly at war here,” Marta said quietly, running her fingers over a fragment of an old concrete bunker. “And they have been for a long time.”

No one disagreed. Even the air smelled different here. The smell of damp earth was mixed with smoke, grease, gunpowder, and something else, something elusive that lingered only in places where war had gone on for years.

“Can you sense our people this time?” Lior asked.

Olena, the most sensitive to the mind-link among the four, closed her eyes for only a few seconds.

“Yes,” she answered confidently.

Nayrin immediately turned towards her.

“Are they close?”

“Not very… But much closer than ever before.”

She listened to the strange sensation for a little longer, then slowly shook her head.

“There are many of them. And… they’re looking for someone too.”

Nayrin tried to concentrate, but he could not sense anything nearly as clearly. After Marta’s lessons, he had learned to use his abilities better, but he was still far from having such sensitivity.

Suddenly, Lior, who had just peered over the edge of the slope, crouched sharply.

“Movement.”

Everyone cautiously rose just high enough to see the field. On the far horizon, a long cloud of dust was slowly rising. At first it looked like ordinary heat haze, but within a few seconds the silhouettes of motorcycles began to emerge from it.

There were many of them. At least a dozen. The machines were approaching quickly, easily avoiding craters, jumping over the remains of trenches, and barely slowing down.

“They’re coming this way,” Nayrin said quietly.

No one answered. The four silently watched the riders, trying to understand who they might be. Marta merely shook her head.

“I don’t know… They may be looking for us. Or they may be after someone else entirely.”

The uncertainty did not last long. An armoured vehicle suddenly appeared from behind a bend in a dirt road a little farther away. It was racing towards the motorcyclists at high speed, raising a thick cloud of dust behind it.

“More of them…” Olena whispered.

The vehicle turned sharply across the road, blocking the way. The motorcyclists did not brake either. For a moment, it seemed the two groups were about to collide, but just then a gunner rose above the armoured vehicle’s roof.

A short burst of automatic fire rang out. The first bullets struck the ground directly in front of the lead motorcycle, forcing its rider to swerve sharply. Its wheels slid over the loose earth, the machine skidded and overturned, rolling over several times.

The other riders immediately scattered in different directions. Gunfire thundered in response. Bullets peppered the vehicle’s armour, throwing off sparks and clouds of dust, but the gunner did not even try to fire continuously. He used short, carefully measured bursts, each time forcing the attackers to change direction. Meanwhile, the driver never stayed in one place. He kept manoeuvring, using craters, hills, and fragments of concrete shelters as natural cover.

The fight lasted only a few minutes. Two more motorcycles were left motionless in the field, after which the remaining attackers abruptly turned around and began to retreat, quickly disappearing behind a hill.

The armoured vehicle’s engine was still growling softly, but the shooting had stopped. Its door opened. A man in a worn military uniform stepped out. He carefully surveyed the field, as though he knew someone nearby was watching him.

Marta was the first to sense a familiar presence. She slowly exhaled and gave a faint smile.

“They’re ours.”

Nayrin asked no questions. He simply trusted her.

The four of them climbed out of the ravine one after another and stepped into the open. The soldier noticed them almost immediately. His stern face changed at once into a friendly smile.

“Finally, there you are!” he called. “We’ve been searching this area for two days. Get to the vehicle, quickly! We shouldn’t stay out in the open for long.”

Within a few minutes, the armoured vehicle was speeding along the broken road towards a military base.

On the way, the driver and the gunner talked constantly about life in this world. It knew almost no peace. Wars lasted for decades, alliances were formed and dissolved, front lines shifted constantly, and most people lived on heavily fortified military bases, from which combat and search units set out every day.

They spoke most of all about the Major. Many years earlier, he had led the first unit made up of people from their home plane. Since then, the unit had continued to grow, and locals had begun joining them. Some came seeking protection from skilled fighters; others were skilled fighters themselves. There were now many families here, and children had even been born.

Despite the fact that such a powerful group could have settled in the best location and easily repelled attacks from almost any other group, the Major still ordered them to keep moving. He sent out mobile search teams, tried to reach others through the mind-link, and built an intelligence network among other factions.

Everything they did—even the heavily fortified forts they built after each relocation—served one purpose: to find those who had survived and come from their home plane. And their efforts were bearing fruit. Through some unknown coincidence, a great many people who had survived the nuclear catastrophe had ended up on this very plane.

To the driver and gunner, these four were simply the latest newcomers they had managed to find, and this time they had found them more quickly than usual. It never occurred to them that these people had not been waiting to be found. They had learned to open paths between worlds themselves. For now, the four travellers decided to keep that to themselves, trusting Nayrin, who had shared his concerns with them through the mind-link. Something about the general mind-link of the base made him uneasy, though he could not yet explain exactly what.

The armoured vehicle continued bouncing along the broken dirt road for a long time before the first signs of the base appeared ahead. At first, Nayrin noticed only the tall communication antennas rising above the treetops. Then the dark silhouettes of enormous armoured vehicles began to emerge from behind a hill. They stood in a tight ring, wheel to wheel, forming an improvised wall that could withstand more than one assault.

There was not a single gap between the vehicles. Every space had been sealed with heavy steel plates welded together, and above them rose machine-gun positions, observation platforms, and searchlights. Several rows of barbed wire stretched in front of the fortification, along with anti-tank obstacles and wide belts of minefields marked with red flags. Everything suggested that the people living here had long since stopped thinking of war as something that would end soon. They built their temporary camp as though they were preparing to defend it for years.

Silent guards met the armoured vehicle at the main entrance. They recognised it immediately, raised the heavy metal barrier, and let the vehicle column pass without delay. Only then could the four see the life of the base itself. Work was going on everywhere. Some people were repairing vehicles in the open air, others were carrying crates of ammunition and food supplies, mechanics nearby were dismantling the engine of a large truck, and a little farther away medics were setting up a field dressing station. Despite the constant activity, no one ran or shouted. Everyone knew their job, and this sense of order made an even stronger impression than the fortifications themselves.

As the vehicle slowly rolled along the camp’s central street, Nayrin noticed a spacious training ground where several dozen people were exercising. At first, it looked like ordinary hand-to-hand combat, but within a minute it became clear that there was nothing ordinary about these people. A young man flipped a broad-shouldered fighter twice his weight with a single shove. Another cleared an armoured personnel carrier in one jump without even touching its hull with his hands. Two men were practising with an enormous steel beam that four ordinary people would probably struggle even to lift. The locals had clearly already mastered the strength that had helped the four travellers survive in their worlds.

The armoured vehicle stopped beside the largest tent in the centre of the camp. Unlike the others, it had a wooden frame, was covered with several layers of canvas, and was additionally protected by sandbags. Two armed guards stood by the entrance, but as soon as the engine was switched off, the tent flap moved aside and a man of about fifty stepped out.

He was tall, broad-shouldered, and stood remarkably straight, although his short hair was already thick with grey. Deep lines around his eyes and across his forehead marked him as a man long accustomed to nights without rest. Almost all insignia had worn off his faded field uniform, except for the small major’s shoulder boards and a neatly sewn patch bearing the emblem of their home world. His gaze was alert but not harsh. It showed the habit of assessing people quickly, along with genuine pleasure at every new encounter.

“Four more at last,” he said with a smile, holding out his hand. “Welcome home. I’m the Major—that’s what they call me here. I’ve been commanding this base for eight years now. Come inside. You probably have more questions than strength left.”

Inside, the tent was much more spacious than it had seemed from the outside. Maps, field radios, fortification plans, and dozens of neatly arranged folders covered the tables. Wooden weapon cabinets stood along the walls, and a small stove was burning quietly in the corner.

The Major did not immediately bombard his guests with questions. First, he ordered hot food and tea to be brought in, and only then sat down opposite them.

“We’ll ask questions gradually. First, tell me the most important thing. How did you manage to survive on your own for so long?”

Nayrin exchanged a brief glance with Lior.

“We weren’t alone,” he answered calmly. “For a while, we lived with another unit. But a few days ago, they were defeated. We only just managed to escape the pursuit.”

The Major sighed heavily and nodded slowly.

“So it was the people from the Black Hills region… We’ve already received reports. A few weeks ago, one of our allied groups was almost completely wiped out by motorcycle units like the ones you saw today. Things like that happen constantly here. Today they destroy you; tomorrow you destroy someone else. If you want to survive in this world, you have to learn to accept losses without illusions.”

He fell silent briefly, as though giving his guests time to get used to those words.

“That’s exactly why we joined forces. At first, we only wanted to find our own people. We thought that if we gathered everyone together, that would be enough. But the years pass, and the war does not end. We realised that we could spend our entire lives defending ourselves, or we could finally change this world. Our task has long since grown beyond finding people from our home. We want to unite all human factions under a single command, put an end to the endless wars, and bring order here. If we don’t do it, no one will.”

Lior listened carefully, then asked almost casually:

“Have you ever considered… that other worlds might exist?”

The Major actually smiled.

“Honestly? Not once. We have enough to deal with here. We still don’t know why everyone ended up in this place. Some call it a miracle, others a punishment from God, and still others look for scientific explanations. But what difference does it make? We’re alive. And if we’re alive, we have to build the future right here.”

He spoke with complete sincerity, and that was what made Nayrin most uneasy. The Major was hiding nothing. He believed in his own position so firmly that he did not even consider the possibility of another answer. The strange inner sensation that had stayed with Nayrin since the journey stirred faintly again, but he still could not explain it.

After a long conversation, the Major stood up.

“That’s enough for today. You need to rest. Tomorrow you’ll meet the others, see how we live, and then we’ll decide what you’ll do next.”

They were led to a separate tent not far from headquarters. Inside stood four simple army beds, a folding table, several wooden crates used as cupboards, and a kerosene lamp that softly lit the canvas walls. After many days on the road, even such accommodation seemed like luxury.

Lior silently unfastened his gear. Marta sat down tiredly on the edge of a bed, while Olena had barely spoken all evening. She sat by the entrance, looking through the partly open flap at the campfires, as though listening for something distant and unreachable.

Nayrin lay down last. Sleep would not come. The Major’s words, the fighters’ training, and the strange confidence of people who had lived here for many years kept returning to his mind. Everything seemed to fit, yet some almost imperceptible detail stubbornly refused to let him rest. He could not understand what made him so uneasy that he wanted to conceal from the Major both their new method of mind-link communication and their discovery of portals. The decision had not come from logical conclusions, but from a quiet inner feeling that had never let him down.

“Tomorrow,” Lior said quietly, as though he had read Nayrin’s thoughts. “If nothing changes, we’ll tell him.”

Nayrin simply nodded.

The lamp was extinguished. Outside the tent, the voices of the guards could be heard for a long time, machinery engines rumbled, and distant commands from the night patrols rang out. The base went on with its familiar life, preparing for another day of war, while the four travellers finally closed their eyes, leaving all the answers until morning.
