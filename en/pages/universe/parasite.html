FORCES AND PHENOMENA

The Parasite

An entity that feeds on human suffering and influences people

DESCRIPTION IN PROGRESS · SOME MECHANISMS REQUIRE CLARIFICATION

NATURE AND ORIGIN

An entity of unknown origin

The Parasite is a distinct entity that feeds on human pain and suffering. It can influence a person's mind and body, while maintaining connections with many people at the same time.

According to the current account, the Parasite separated from a maternal entity while still formless and almost blind. What this maternal entity is, whether the Parasite belongs to a particular species, and whether similar entities exist remain unknown. Its origin is a mystery.

At first, it could only influence individual actions to a limited degree. It later learned to control a host's body and behaviour completely while feeding on the suffering of that person's consciousness. As its power grew, it became capable of extending its influence over increasing numbers of people and entire societies.

The Parasite can survive after losing its human source of nourishment, although it becomes much weaker. In that state, it was able to sustain itself on the pain of animals. The exact limits of this ability are unknown.

HOST AND CONTROLLED PEOPLE

One entity — many connections

Direct host

The Parasite can have only one direct host: a person whose body it uses for its own existence and interaction with the world.

Controlled people

At the same time, the Parasite can connect to a large number of other people and influence their actions without being physically present in their bodies.

Controlled people do not necessarily lose their own consciousness. In some cases, a person retains their identity and remains aware of what they are doing. In other cases, consciousness may be lost or suppressed. The exact states and the conditions that determine them have not yet been established.

A direct host should not be confused with people connected to the Parasite. These are different forms of interaction with the human world.

CONNECTION AND CONTROL

A mechanism yet to be understood

The Parasite can maintain numerous connections with people simultaneously. In some cases, these connections appear as thin threads extending from the entity to the people.

Through these connections, the Parasite can influence people's behaviour and control them. The nature of the connection itself, however, has not yet been explained. It is unknown how it is established, how it is maintained, what range it has, or whether the number of connected people is limited.

It is also unclear whether the Parasite can transmit information between connected people in a way similar to the mind-link. Similarities in some effects do not prove that the mechanisms are identical.

After a group of people was isolated from external contact, some of them lost consciousness even though their physical condition remained stable. This observation suggests that these people depended on external control, but it does not explain the mechanism itself.

NOURISHMENT AND POWER

Its food is human suffering

The Parasite's main known source of nourishment is human pain and suffering. It does not merely exploit existing tragedies; it can cause them by influencing people's decisions and actions.

The more suffering it consumes, the stronger it becomes. Its growing power gradually expands its capabilities: from minor influence over individual actions to complete control of a person, and eventually to the control of large groups.

It is possible to sever the Parasite from human suffering without destroying the entity itself. Such an intervention weakens it dramatically, but does not automatically kill it.

MOVEMENT BETWEEN PLANS

Using existing transitions

The Parasite can move between Plans through transitions that already exist. It cannot create new transitions by its own will. Access to a route into another Sphere depends on the existing transition structure.

In the draft account of its history, this ability to move appeared after the first world had been emptied. The exact moment when the ability emerged and its mechanism still require clarification.

It has not yet been decided whether the Parasite needs a direct host in order to move between Plans. It is also unknown whether it can exist and travel without a host.

GUARDIANS OF THE CENTRAL PLANS

A mechanism of protection

A Central Plan has its own protective mechanism, connected with the appearance of a Guardian. How this mechanism is activated, why it selects a particular person, and whether it works in the same way across different Spheres remain unknown.

The Parasite's history points to a connection between a Guardian's death and the possibility of reaching a new Sphere. The Parasite concluded that it had to kill the Guardian itself for such a route to appear. Whether this is a universal rule, how the mechanism works, and whether this condition is connected to humanity's destruction still need to be determined.

The Guardians, their abilities and the Central Plan's protective mechanism are still being examined. Events involving one Guardian should not automatically be treated as rules applying to all Guardians.

HISTORY OF THE PARASITE

From its first hunger to a hunter of worlds

The following is a literary retelling of the history described in the draft passage. “The Lord” is used as a provisional designation for this entity, not as a finally approved canonical name.

Expand the historical account

It appeared at the edge of the world, in a small village whose people did not know that something alien had been born among them. Having separated from its maternal entity, it was formless and almost blind. The world had no shape until it instinctively attached itself to a passing woman. Through her eyes, it saw the earth, the sky and human faces for the first time.

Sight did not satisfy its hunger. It felt an emptiness it could not name and searched for something to fill it. Its first push into the woman's mind was almost imperceptible: a careless step, a fall, a broken leg. Then came pain. That pain became its first food.

Months passed. Crops failed, game disappeared, and people fell ill or suffered strange injuries. Misfortune drove the villagers from their homes. When the village stood empty, the elder was the last to leave. The entity that had learned to feed on human misery went with him.

Before long, an entire country was considered cursed. Accidental injuries and small tragedies were no longer enough. The Parasite discovered full control: it could command a person's body, movements and words, while leaving the person's consciousness fully aware of the horror unfolding. The victim could witness their own actions but could no longer stop them. That helplessness became another source of nourishment.

It soon realised that controlling a ruler would be more effective than influencing dozens of ordinary people. By taking the king, it gained a kingdom as an instrument. Armies marched against neighbouring lands, borders expanded, and conquered territories became vast fields in which suffering could be multiplied and harvested. Human prosperity meant nothing to it. Only pain, fear and the number of those capable of feeling them mattered.

Conquest was followed by cults of blood sacrifice. They spread with the armies and brought new waves of agony. But the Parasite killed faster than people could be born. Once all the land had fallen under its control, only one human remained on the planet: a young man whose body became the entity's final refuge. The Parasite tormented him into old age. When the last human host died, hunger gave way to panic for the first time.

Then came a discovery: the entity had not perished. It could leave the emptied world and open its first way to another Plan. There were people there again — countless opportunities to feed. The second world fell faster than the first. More followed. Century after century, the Parasite travelled along available routes between Plans, emptying one world after another, until it was stopped.

A young woman came to its palace. She destroyed the body it was using and severed it from human suffering. Without its usual source of nourishment, the Parasite lost almost all its strength. It did not die, but became weak and nearly helpless. At first, only small animals — chickens and mice — sustained it. Later it drew strength from random pain and misfortune, and gradually learned to cause suffering again.

It remembered the woman as the Guardian. Many years later, when she had grown old and lost the force that once protected her, the Parasite found her again. It had not forgotten its defeat and turned the final years of her life into a prolonged reckoning. After her death, a wider picture opened before it: not one world or even one sequence of Plans, but entire Spheres connected in a complex structure.

The hunt resumed. The Parasite moved from Plan to Plan, emptying entire branches and eventually every world it could reach. When the Plans of all known branches had been exhausted, it remembered a thin branch it had once dismissed as unimportant. It became the only way forward. The Parasite left behind a Sphere in which no people remained.

In the next Sphere it found advanced civilisations. At first it destroyed them one by one, but soon learned that slow extermination yielded less strength than catastrophes that killed millions in a short time. It began provoking planetary disasters, including nuclear conflicts, and experimenting with ways to turn the destruction of civilisations into nourishment. Suffering became not only food, but a variety of flavours.

Then new Guardians began to appear. The Parasite killed the first near its residence, but no new route opened. Others followed. Only after several such encounters did it conclude that a passage to another Sphere opened when it personally killed a Guardian. It trained, studied its opponents and eventually defeated one in a brief duel. A new route appeared on its map of worlds.

The next Sphere was different: it had no advanced civilisations ready to be consumed. The Parasite began accelerating their development, pushing societies towards greatness so that it could destroy them later and harvest greater suffering. But no Guardian appeared for a long time, and without one the Parasite did not know how to open a route to new hunting grounds. Hunger gradually overcame caution. It decided to attack the Central Plan, risking the possibility that no new transition would ever become available.

There it found a Guardian in an exosuit. The Guardian was struggling against the control of two psi-operators, while the Parasite imagined breaking his will over years. But it decided not to wait and chose a quick kill. At first it saw calm and curiosity in the Guardian's eyes. That angered it. It put aside its sword and took a dagger, intending to inflict pain before death. Yet in the final moment, the Guardian's gaze showed something unexpected: not fear, but pity and understanding.
