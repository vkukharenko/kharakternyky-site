05 / PLAN DESTRUCTION

The destruction of a planetary body can gradually deprive a Plan of its transition

Transition degradation does not necessarily begin immediately after a catastrophe. Even after a planet has been severely damaged, its transition may remain stable for decades.

As the planet continues to break apart, portals become increasingly difficult to open and maintain. At first they require greater effort. Later, ordinary portals large enough for people become impossible, although very small portals may remain accessible for probes. Eventually, the transition becomes entirely inaccessible.

Degradation may affect the entire Plan rather than a specific point or established route. The remaining mass of a large planetary body may influence transition stability, but the exact mechanism and the critical mass required to sustain a transition have not been determined.

Branch decay

If the destruction of a planetary body causes a branch to lose its connection to the Central Plan, a process known as branch “withering” begins.

First, the planets on that branch gradually begin to lose mass. Matter disappears without debris, gas emissions or other signs of ordinary destruction. The process begins with the most distant Plans and gradually spreads towards the destroyed Plan.

After mass loss begins, transitions gradually degrade until travel between the affected Plans is no longer possible. What happens to the planets after access to them is lost remains unknown. The physical mechanism behind the disappearance of matter has also not been established.
