BIOLOGICAL SPECIES

Shadow Stalker

A pack-hunting predator from another Plan. A natural enemy of great cats and a hunter of humans.

OVERVIEW

A predator adapted to hunting powerful prey

The Shadow Stalker is an agile terrestrial predator native to another Plan. Its main advantages are not armour or extraordinary strength, but speed, agility, short-distance endurance and coordinated action by several individuals.

On its native Plan, Shadow Stalkers regard great cats as ordinary natural prey. They also hunt humans: people are neither unfamiliar competitors nor a reason to avoid settlements. When conditions allow, they may also prey on wild and domestic animals.

Shadow Stalkers have no natural enemies other than great cats. This helped their population grow and made the species a major threat to other inhabitants of its native Plan.

APPEARANCE AND PHYSIOLOGY

Body structure

Body lengthApproximately 100–140 cm, excluding the tail
Shoulder heightApproximately 45–65 cm
MassApproximately 35–60 kg
LocomotionQuadrupedal; capable of rapid acceleration, jumping and crossing uneven terrain
BuildElongated flexible body, low centre of gravity, powerful hind limbs and long forelimbs with strong claws
Skin and coveringDense skin and a short fur-like or fibrous coat whose colouring helps it blend into its surroundings
HeadRelatively small and elongated, with strong jaws adapted to gripping and tearing prey

A Shadow Stalker has no heavy natural armour. Its defence relies on mobility, changing the direction of an attack and avoiding direct confrontation with a stronger opponent.

SENSES

A hunter of twilight and ambush

Vision

Good at detecting movement and adapted to twilight. It does not see equally well in complete darkness.

Hearing

Sensitive hearing helps it locate moving prey and detect approaching animals or people.

Smell

Helps it follow tracks, distinguish the scent of animals and humans, and return to places where prey was found.

Reaction

It reacts quickly to changes in a target's movement. It is most dangerous at close range, where it can change direction suddenly and attack from the side or rear.

BEHAVIOUR AND PACKS

Coordination without a rigid hierarchy

Shadow Stalkers usually hunt in groups of approximately four to eight. A pack does not necessarily have a permanent leader: during a hunt, the animals coordinate through body movements, sounds and scent signals. Roles may change depending on the terrain and the prey's behaviour.

A pack may pursue its target, force it to change direction, divide its attention and attack from several sides. If the prey proves too dangerous, the Stalkers retreat, observe and may attack again when conditions improve.

Individuals can survive without a pack. Solitary animals rely more often on ambush, choose smaller prey and avoid open confrontation with great cats. Solitary life is possible, but a pack has a much better chance of bringing down a large animal.

REPRODUCTION AND POPULATION

Rapid population recovery

Reproduction is seasonal. The main mating period falls during the favourable part of the year, when food is plentiful and conditions allow young to be raised. As a working biological model, a female may produce one or two litters per year, usually with three to five young in each litter.

Young animals begin hunting independently relatively early and reach sexual maturity at approximately one to one and a half years of age. High fertility, early maturity and the absence of other natural enemies allow the population to recover quickly after losses.

These figures form a working biological model. They explain how Shadow Stalkers could become a major problem on their native Plan, without making them invulnerable: population growth still depends on food, breeding conditions and hunting losses.

HUNTING AND THREAT

Why they are dangerous on the surface

In open terrain, Shadow Stalkers use speed, camouflage and the ability to attack from different directions. The whole pack does not necessarily rush the target at once: some may distract it while others try to approach from the side or rear.

A great cat can kill a Shadow Stalker and has an advantage in mass and strength in a direct confrontation. However, a lone cat remains vulnerable to a pack, especially if the animals attack in turns, try to exhaust it or reach unprotected areas.

An unprotected human is also prey. A Shadow Stalker can attack suddenly, using cover, poor visibility or a victim's inattention. It has no supernatural resistance to bullets: small-arms hits can seriously injure or kill it.

On the surface

Speed, manoeuvrability, ambushes and attacks from several directions give Shadow Stalkers an advantage over unorganised groups of people.

Underground

Narrow corridors and rooms make it harder for packs to manoeuvre around defenders. Infantry equipped with gilkomets can control passages and kill the animals if they detect them in time.

NATURAL ENEMIES

Great cats — their only natural opponent

Great cats are the only animals that naturally oppose Shadow Stalkers. Hunting them shaped part of the Stalkers' predatory behaviour: they can attack strong, fast prey, search for weak points and work as a group.

An individual human or cat may survive an encounter with a Shadow Stalker, but that does not guarantee victory. The outcome depends on surprise, the number of predators, the terrain and the ability to use weapons or armour.

THE TURNING POINT

Human and great cat as one fighting unit

The turning point on the native Plan came when riders stopped treating the great cat merely as a mount and learned to act with it as a single unit.

The cat contributes acute hearing and smell, rapid reactions, manoeuvrability and the ability to move through difficult terrain. The human contributes strategic thinking, target selection, modern weapons and an understanding of the wider situation. Rider and animal learn to read each other's movements and act in coordination without losing their own natural advantages.

Modern armour for great cats reduces the risk of fatal injuries but does not make them invulnerable and must not restrict their mobility. The decisive advantage is the combination of two different ways of perceiving and fighting.

ARRIVAL ON ANOTHER PLAN

A threat spreading into a new world

After reaching another Plan, Shadow Stalkers retain their main hunting instincts. They continue to hunt humans and animals, adapt quickly to new terrain and can enter underground shelters if they find accessible openings.

In underground cities, their main advantage is reduced: confined spaces make it harder for a pack to manoeuvre and attack from several directions. Organised infantry equipped with gilkomets can stop them in corridors and rooms.

The species' high sensitivity to ionising radiation may become an additional biological factor limiting its spread. This does not mean that any contact with radiation causes instant death: the consequences depend on the dose received and the duration of exposure.
