Universe structure · Interworld navigation

Spheres of Plans

Central Plans, portal branches, branching paths and a unified system for cataloguing planetary worlds.

01 · General model

What Is a Sphere of Plans?

A Sphere of Plans is an integrated system of worlds connected around a Central Plan.

Branches extend from the Central Plan. Each branch consists of individual Plans connected by transitions. Branches can split, creating new routes. Every Plan has a defined place in this structure and a route back to the Central Plan.

Sequential transitions

To reach a distant Plan, travellers must pass through the intermediate Plans. An ordinary transition cannot skip them.

Limits of a Sphere

The maximum number of Plans in a Sphere is unknown and may be unlimited. However, each individual branch has its own terminal Plan.

02 · Central nodes

Three Known Central Plans

By the end of the second book, three Central Plans are known: the Zero Plan, the First Plan and the Second Plan. The Central Plans are directly connected to one another. Each is the centre of its own branch system.

Z0 · Zero Plan

The central world from which branches coded Z0 are numbered.

Z1 · First Plan

The second of the known Central Plans, directly connected to the other central nodes.

Z2 · Second Plan

The third known Central Plan and the centre of its own branch system.

Inter-Sphere connection

The Spheres are connected by transitions between their Central Plans. Other forms of connection are addressed separately.

03 · Portal network

Branch structure

A branch begins at a Central Plan. Individual Plans are located along its length and connected by sequential transitions. A line may split at specific nodes; each new branch begins at an existing Plan. Branches can split again.

Movement rule: a characterник can move only to the next Plan along their branch. To travel farther, they must pass through the intermediate nodes in sequence.

04 · Coding standard

Planetary Worlds Information Package

For cataloguing and navigation between worlds, characterник expeditions use a unified planetary-world code. It identifies the central world, branch, sequential Plan number, world class and danger level.

Z0-15-387-M05-D4
Z[Centre]-[Branch]-[Number]-[Class]-[Level]
Z0Central world
15Branch number
387Plan number
M05World class
D4Danger level

01. Central world

Z0 — Zero Plan; Z1 — First Plan; Z2 — Second Plan. The first block identifies the Central Plan from which the branches are numbered.

02. Branch

The second number is the portal branch number from the central world. For example, Z0-15 means the fifteenth branch from Z0. Branches may split into further branches. To keep the code manageable, the standard notation records only the first branch from the centre.

03. Plan number

The third block is the sequential number of the Plan discovered on that branch. It indicates discovery order, not distance.

04. World class

The fourth block identifies the world category in the catalogue: Living, Dead, Extreme or Unknown.

05. Danger level

The fifth block indicates the current risk assessment for access. It may be revised as new research becomes available.

06 · Risk assessment

Danger level

The fifth block of the code identifies the current danger level of a world. This is an operational assessment for expeditions, not an immutable property; it may be revised after further research.

D1Safe world
D2Limited danger
D3Dangerous world
D4Deadly danger
D5Access prohibited

07 · Portal status

World access status

Access status is recorded separately from the five-part world code. It describes the current state of the route or portal, not the world’s classification.

OPENAccess open
LIMITAccess restricted
SEALEDPortal closed
LOSTRoute lost
UNSTABLEUnstable portal
Important: the existence of a planetary code does not guarantee that a world has been fully explored. A significant proportion of known worlds are classified as M or X. The code records the characteristics known at the time; it must not be confused with a statement that the world has been completely investigated.

The coding system is used by characterник expeditions to maintain world maps, plan transitions and assess risks.
