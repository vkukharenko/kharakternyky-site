UNIVERSE REFERENCE · ROBOTIC SYSTEMS

R-17

Universal modular robotic platform. A six-legged support machine designed for battlefield operations, difficult terrain and environments where deploying a human operator would be too risky.

01 / PURPOSE

One platform, many tasks

R-17 is neither a mechanical infantry soldier nor a replacement for a human fighter. It is a universal robotic support system for a unit. Its main advantage is a shared base chassis that can be reconfigured for a specific task using interchangeable limbs, tools, sensors and cargo modules.

Its low armoured body and six articulated supports provide stability and allow it to move through rubble, trenches, damaged buildings and uneven terrain. The platform can redistribute its load between supports and retain limited mobility after an individual limb fails.

02 / ARCHITECTURE

Platform construction

Central chassis

A protected low-profile frame housing distributed power, computing, communications and sensors. Functional sections are isolated so local damage does not automatically disable the entire machine.

Six universal mounts

Mounting points accept different limbs and equipment. The base configuration uses six walking limbs; selected units can be replaced with manipulators or specialised work modules.

Power system

Battery modules are distributed through the chassis. The segmented layout supports balancing, maintenance and isolation of damaged components.

Computing and sensors

Local computing processes sensor data, stabilises movement, builds a spatial model of the surroundings and supports network interaction. Core functions do not rely exclusively on a continuous external connection.

Modularity is the defining feature of R-17: one platform changes its working configuration instead of requiring a different machine for every task.
03 / CONFIGURATIONS

Four principal roles

Reconnaissance

Optical and thermal sensors, perimeter observation, motion detection, 3D mapping, route surveillance and information relay to the unit.

Engineering

Manipulators and interchangeable tools for clearing passages, moving debris, inspecting damaged structures, assisting repairs and checking hazardous areas.

Transport

Carrying equipment, ammunition, batteries, medical supplies and cargo. In a suitable configuration, it can assist with casualty evacuation from dangerous areas.

Combat support

Additional observation, communications relay, equipment delivery, sensor coverage and engineering support. R-17 operates as part of a unit rather than independently deciding to use lethal force.

04 / CONTROL

Three modes of interaction

Autonomous

The platform performs an assigned task: follows a route, observes, maps terrain, transports cargo or returns to a service point.

Operator-assisted

The operator provides a direction or intent while onboard systems calculate movement, maintain balance, avoid obstacles and monitor limb loads.

Thought-link control

A fighter can receive the platform's sensor information and guide its movement or manipulators through a thought-link bracelet. R-17 becomes an additional point of perception and a set of working limbs for the operator.

Network interaction

Data exchange with fighters, other robots and command systems. When needed, the platform can act as a local relay or an additional sensor node.

05 / CAPABILITIES AND LIMITS

Resilient, not invulnerable

A segmented chassis, redundant power, self-diagnostics and fault isolation allow some functions to remain available after damage. However, R-17 is not invulnerable: damage to actuators, sensors, the chassis or communications reduces its capabilities. Difficult terrain, excessive load or energy shortages may force it into a degraded mode or end its task.

Additional uses include search and rescue, hazardous-area monitoring, radiation and chemical sensing, underground inspection, infrastructure checks and assistance in maintaining other robotic systems.

07 / TECHNOLOGICAL CONTEXT

How R-17 differs from Zero Plan robots

Robots are already a familiar part of the technological environment on the Zero Plan. There are transport, repair, service, research and other specialised systems, each designed for a defined range of tasks.

Those machines, however, were not adapted for battlefield conditions and did not share a modular architecture that could quickly change a platform's purpose. A new task generally required a different specialised model.

R-17 addresses that gap. Its innovation is not the existence of a robot itself, but the combination of battlefield adaptation, a universal chassis, interchangeable limbs, unified control and network interaction.

Canon distinction: robots already exist on the Zero Plan, mostly as separate specialised non-combat systems. R-17 is a new universal modular platform adapted for wartime conditions.
