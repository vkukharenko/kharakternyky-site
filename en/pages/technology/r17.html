01 / PURPOSE

One platform, many tasks

R-17 is a universal robotic support platform for a unit. It carries one fighter, equipment and cargo; provides observation, engineering assistance and combat support. R-17 does not replace a fighter and is not an autonomous infantry soldier: it is an additional mobile resource for the unit.

Its defining feature is the combination of six-legged walking and wheeled travel. On roads and firm surfaces, the platform uses its wheels. Among rubble, on stairs and over difficult terrain, it switches to six-legged locomotion.

02 / CONSTRUCTION

One chassis, two ways to move

The central armoured chassis forms the core of the crab-like layout. Six locomotion limbs attach around its perimeter — front, sides and rear — forming three pairs of supports. Two separate manipulators are mounted at the front of the chassis, one on the left and one on the right. They are not locomotion limbs and do not use the limb mounting points. The central chassis houses battery modules, computing, communications, sensors and control systems.

Four corner limbs carry retractable wheel modules. The two middle limbs remain as support limbs and help overcome obstacles and stabilise the chassis. In wheeled mode, the corner limbs form a rectangular wheelbase while the middle pair is raised. In walking mode, the wheels retract and all six limbs operate as supports.

03 / MODULES AND ARMAMENT

Weapons and additional equipment

Two needle guns are mounted on dedicated structural points on the central chassis. The heavy module is intended for heavy penetrators; the light module uses lighter ammunition and carries a larger reserve. Mounting the guns on the chassis leaves all six limb mounts available.

Two manipulators are mounted at the front of the central chassis, one on the left and one on the right. They perform auxiliary tasks such as inspection, moving selected debris and handling equipment. Each has a dedicated mounting point and neither replaces any of the six locomotion limbs. A seat for one fighter is a standard part of the platform.

04 / CONTROL

Three control modes

Autonomous mode: the platform performs assigned tasks, plans its route, observes and returns to a service point.

Operator-assisted mode: the operator provides a direction or intent while onboard systems stabilise movement, avoid obstacles and monitor loads. Thought-link control lets a fighter receive R-17 sensor information and guide its movement and manipulator. Network interaction is not a separate control mode; it is the data link connecting fighters, other platforms and command systems.

05 / POWER SYSTEM

Batteries and energy budget

The nominal battery capacity is approximately 80 kWh. At the design target of 500 Wh/kg, the battery pack weighs approximately 160 kg. This is a target enabled by Zero Plan technology, not a specification for present-day batteries.

A 15% protected reserve is excluded from routine use, leaving approximately 68 kWh available. Segmented modules can be isolated or replaced individually. Range, stationary endurance and intensive combat operation draw from the same energy budget and cannot all reach their maximum values at once.

Battery protection

The battery modules are housed inside the central armoured chassis in several separate protected compartments. They do not form the platform’s outer surface and are not directly exposed to incoming fire. Internal structural partitions, shock-isolated mounts and thermal insulation reduce the risk of battery damage from impacts, fragments and chassis deformation.

Each segment has independent monitoring of temperature, voltage and condition. If a module is penetrated, overheats or short-circuits, the control system disconnects it from the main power bus. A fire-suppression system contains the incident inside that compartment and limits propagation to neighbouring modules. Damage to one segment reduces available capacity but does not necessarily disable the entire battery pack.

Autonomous energy replenishment

R-17 has two auxiliary self-charging systems. The first uses thermoelectric generators integrated into the heat-removal loop. They convert part of the temperature difference between heated components and the cooling circuit into electricity and return it to the battery. The system recovers some heat that would otherwise be dissipated, but it cannot offset the platform’s energy consumption during active movement or combat.

The second system consists of solar photovoltaic panels. Protected flat cells are integrated into the upper surfaces of the chassis and can generate power while the platform is moving or stationary. For extended stops, additional folding panels can be deployed to increase the collection area. They fold into armoured recesses before travel or combat. The panels are divided into sections so damage to one part does not disable the entire array.

Both sources can operate simultaneously and feed the battery pack through a charge controller. Actual energy recovery depends on sunlight, thermal conditions and the platform’s current load. These systems help maintain charge and extend endurance; they do not replace an external charging station.

06 / THERMAL MANAGEMENT

Heat and detectability

The greatest cooling demand occurs during active walking: losses in actuators, gearboxes and electronics heat internal components. Wheeled travel reduces locomotion energy and therefore lowers thermal load, but it does not make the platform cold or invisible.

Mechanical joint locks hold the limbs while stationary without continuously powering the actuators. Sensors, computing and communications remain active. Heat is managed by a liquid cooling loop and heat exchangers. Exact heat output and infrared signature depend on operating conditions and are not stated as universal fixed figures.

07 / OPERATING MODES

From travel to stationary watch

Travel mode uses the wheels on roads and firm surfaces. Difficult-terrain mode uses six-legged locomotion among rubble, on stairs and over uneven ground.

Combat mode combines observation, coordination with the unit and human-supervised weapon operation. In stationary watch mode, mechanical locks reduce actuator power demand. In transport mode, the platform carries one fighter and additional equipment within its payload limit.

09 / LIMITATIONS

Capabilities and limitations

R-17 cannot simultaneously achieve maximum range, long stationary endurance and intensive combat operation. Walking through rubble consumes far more energy than wheeled travel. Wheeled operation is limited on soft ground, steep slopes and unstable surfaces.

Damage to limbs, sensors, chassis or communications reduces the platform's capabilities. If energy becomes insufficient, R-17 switches to a reduced-function mode or ends its task. The decision to use lethal force remains with the human operator.
